# HPE — Hewlett Packard Enterprise Strategy

## Thesis Summary

Hewlett Packard Enterprise is not legacy HP. It is a steady enterprise infrastructure business with sticky customers, long sales cycles, and a growing recurring revenue platform in GreenLake — HPE's consumption-based hybrid cloud offering. The networking segment has become a genuine growth engine following the Juniper integration. In fiscal Q1 2026, HPE posted $9.3B in revenue (+18% YoY), 36% GAAP gross margins sharply improved YoY, and $0.7B in free cash flow — up ~$1.6B year over year. Improving margins and rising FCF materially reduce the "unexpected impairment" risk that makes short puts dangerous.

The market still buckets HPE as old tech — low-growth hardware, enterprise laggard — which ignores GreenLake's recurring revenue conversion, the networking growth engine post-Juniper, and the fact that AI infrastructure spend flows through servers, storage, and networking, not just semiconductors. HPE trades at single-digit to low-teens earnings multiples despite margin expansion and FCF growth. A re-rating does not require AI hype multiples or explosive revenue growth — only continued margin stability, consistent FCF conversion, and management confidence expressed through dividends and buybacks. This is a slow-burn re-rating thesis: similar in archetype to MSFT but lower-octane. You get paid via premium while the market slowly figures it out.

The options profile makes this structurally attractive. IV runs ~40–45% while realized volatility is materially lower, and HPE options tend to price macro anxiety rather than company-specific risk. This is exactly the setup premium sellers want: boring company, nervous market.

**Why the stock is undervalued:**
- Market perception as legacy hardware ignores GreenLake recurring revenue model and networking growth
- Single-digit to low-teens earnings multiple despite margin expansion and FCF growth
- AI infrastructure spend benefiting servers, storage, and networking — not yet reflected in valuation

**Why the downside is limited:**
- Annual dividend of $0.57 (~2.0–2.3% yield, ~33% payout ratio), raised consistently — creates natural institutional demand on pullbacks
- No binary product risk, no regulatory overhang, no leverage stress, no customer concentration
- GreenLake ARR provides recurring revenue ballast against cyclical pressure
- IV persistently overpriced relative to realized volatility — premium sellers have structural edge

## Strategy Mechanics

The overall approach is cash-flow-first and cadence-driven: HPE is not a hero trade. It is a quietly rewarding serial short-put name where IV is persistently mispriced relative to actual business risk. Run it steadily, accept assignment without stress, and let the wheel do the work.

---

### Strategy 1 — Core Cash-Secured Puts (Foundation)

**What you do:**
- Sell cash-secured puts at 30–45 DTE
- Target delta: ~0.20–0.30
- Strike selection: 15–25% OTM, at levels where assignment is genuinely acceptable

**Why this works:**
- IV runs persistently richer than realized volatility on a range-bound stock
- Dividend support below current price creates natural demand on pullbacks
- Long enterprise sales cycles dampen surprise volatility — the stock does not gap erratically

**When it activates:** Primary strategy — runs on a continuous cadence; no special trigger required.

**Rules:**
- Only sell at strikes you are comfortable owning at; this is a willing-assignment trade
- No need to time earnings — standard IV is already attractive on this name

**Goal:** Generate steady, repeatable premium income on a business with low structural downside risk.

---

### Strategy 2 — Covered Calls After Assignment (Wheel)

**What you do:**
- If assigned shares from Strategy 1, immediately begin selling covered calls at 30–45 DTE
- Conservative strikes: delta ~0.20
- Avoid selling calls through earnings unless IV is unusually elevated

**Why this works:**
- Premium + dividend reduces cost basis steadily over time
- Conservative strikes preserve upside participation if the slow-burn re-rating accelerates
- Range-bound price behavior means covered calls expire worthless at high frequency

**When it activates:** Only upon assignment from Strategy 1.

**Rules:**
- Objective is basis reduction, not capping a re-rate — keep strikes conservative
- Collect the dividend while running covered calls; it compounds the income effect

**Goal:** Convert an assigned position into a continuing income engine while retaining long-term upside exposure to the re-rating thesis.

---

### Strategy 3 — Vertical Put Spread (Advanced / Defined Risk)

**What you do:**
- Sell an ATM or slightly OTM put and buy a put 10–20% lower as protection
- Only execute when net credit exceeds 65–75% of max profit potential (target 65–75% max profit capture)
- Appropriate when IV spikes above the 60–70 percentile range

**Why this works:**
- Defined risk limits downside to spread width minus credit received
- Allows participation in elevated-IV periods without capital commitment to assignment
- Appropriate for clients who do not want equity ownership but want income exposure

**When it activates:** When IV spikes meaningfully above normal levels and defined risk is preferred over assignment.

**Rules:**
- Do not substitute for Strategy 1 for clients comfortable with ownership — the credit is better unhedged
- Only execute if net credit clears the threshold; below that the risk/reward does not justify the trade

**Goal:** Participate in overpriced premium during elevated-fear periods with capped, defined downside.

---

### Strategy 4 — Earnings Premium Capture (HIGH RISK — situational overlay only)

**What you do:**
- Sell a short-dated cash-secured put approximately three days before the earnings announcement
- Strike: 15–20% OTM — conservative enough that assignment remains acceptable if the stock gaps down
- Duration: choose an expiration with at least 2–3 weeks remaining after the earnings date to allow time for recovery if the stock moves against you

**Why this works:**
- IV on HPE inflates into earnings as the market prices uncertainty into option premiums
- HPE earnings tend to be predictable given large enterprise contract visibility and GreenLake ARR as a leading indicator
- Selling ~3 days out captures the peak IV buildup while giving analyst estimate revisions and pre-earnings news flow time to settle — reducing last-minute surprise risk
- Selling the day before leaves no buffer; selling more than a week out sacrifices meaningful IV premium

**When it activates:** Only when all three of the following conditions are met:
1. You have high conviction HPE will meet or beat estimates — supported by GreenLake ARR trends, gross margin trajectory, and management tone in recent investor communications
2. There is no meaningful risk of negative forward guidance that would punish the stock even on a reported beat — no known macro headwinds, tariff exposure shifts, or product cycle disruptions heading into the quarter
3. The chosen expiration has sufficient time after the earnings date to allow the position to recover if the stock temporarily moves against you

**Rules:**
- HIGH RISK — this is a situational overlay, not a core strategy; use only on quarters where conviction is genuinely high across all three conditions
- Small size only — treat as additive income, not a primary position
- Only sell at strikes where assignment is acceptable; if assigned, roll directly into Strategy 2
- Do not execute this strategy if any of the three conditions is uncertain or borderline

**Goal:** Capture elevated pre-earnings IV premium on a predictable, fundamentally sound name — accelerating income in periods where the risk/reward is genuinely favorable.

**You win if:**

You only lose if HPE's fundamentals structurally deteriorate — FCF collapses without a revenue explanation, GreenLake stalls, or leverage increases abruptly. None of those are base-case outcomes.

**Position sizing:**
- Size Strategy 1 puts for comfortable assignment at the chosen strike
- Strategy 3 spreads can be sized more aggressively given defined risk
- This name runs on cadence — consistent smaller size beats concentrated large trades

## Risk Parameters

- **Max loss (cash-secured put):** Strike price minus premium received; practical risk is assignment into a stock whose thesis has broken
- **Max loss (vertical spread):** Width of spread minus net credit received
- **Roll rules:** If short put is tested with more than 14 DTE remaining, evaluate rolling down and out for a net credit; do not roll for a debit unless IV supports it and conviction is high
- **Exit / stop:** Close or roll on a thesis-breaking event — FCF deterioration without revenue shock, abrupt leverage increase, structural margin compression, or GreenLake ARR stall
- **Do not over-manage:** HPE is a low-drama name; resist the urge to close positions early on routine market noise
