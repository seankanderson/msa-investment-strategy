# INTC — Intel Corporation Strategy

## Thesis Summary

Intel is a systemically important US semiconductor manufacturer undergoing one of the most consequential industrial turnarounds in the technology sector. The company's strategic relevance to US semiconductor reshoring, defense supply chains, and advanced domestic fab capacity creates a political and economic floor that typical turnaround stories do not have. The long-term thesis is foundry optionality combined with manufacturing revival — not near-term earnings momentum.

This is a classic "paid to wait" swing thesis, but it is uniquely phase-sensitive. The thesis is intact at the right price level and structurally compromised at the wrong one. Current price behavior — driven by broad market beta expansion and AI sympathy flows rather than earnings re-rating, margin inflection, or concrete foundry customer wins — means discipline around entry level is the primary variable that determines whether this trade works. The name did not break. Price location did.

The historical success on INTC for premium sellers has come from identifying periods of fear-driven vol expansion and stepping in with short puts below obvious support levels — not from chasing strength on low-volume rallies. The edge here is not prediction. It is waiting for the market to do something irrational and getting paid to step in at a level that genuinely compensates for the risk.

**Why the long-term thesis remains intact:**
- Foundry optionality has not disappeared — timeline is long but the strategic opportunity is real
- US semiconductor reshoring tailwind is structural and government-supported
- "Too important to fail" narrative provides downside elasticity not present in typical turnaround names
- Market still discounts execution risk more than total failure — the stock already prices in significant pessimism

**Why current price levels require discipline:**
- Recent rally driven by beta expansion and AI sympathy, not fundamental re-rating
- Gross margin normalization has not yet occurred
- No concrete foundry customer proof points outside subsidized demand
- Low-volume rallies unwind faster than they build — vol expands after price falls, not before

## Entry Criteria

This strategy is explicitly phase-gated. Entry discipline is the primary risk management tool on this name.

**Phase 1 — Hands Off (current elevated levels):**
- Standard income cadence is not appropriate at current elevated spot prices driven by beta drift
- If engagement is required, only wide and deeply conservative puts are appropriate (see Strategy 1A below)
- Do not sell close-to-spot puts during a low-volume, sentiment-driven rally

**Phase 2 — Preferred Entry (after a pullback):**
- Market digestion or macro-driven vol expansion brings INTC back to a prior consolidation base
- IV elevated due to fear or macro contagion rather than company-specific fundamental deterioration
- Put skew steepening on the back of a selloff — front-month IV expanding faster than longer-dated IV
- Volume on down days decreasing relative to up days (distribution signal absent or reversing)
- This is where the bread-and-butter income strategy activates

**Phase 3 — Wheel Activation (after assignment into stabilizing conditions):**
- Only after market finds footing and volatility begins compressing
- Do not begin covered call selling into active market panic — the wheel becomes a grind

## Strategy Mechanics

The overall approach is patience-first: INTC rewards advisors who wait for the market to create an irrational entry level and then sell premium into fear, not into complacency. This is a two-speed name — wrong at the top of a beta rally, excellent at the bottom of a fear-driven reset. The cadence described below reflects the phase-gated structure that matches how this thesis actually works.

---

### Strategy 1A — Wide Conservative Puts at Elevated Levels (Defensive Engagement Only)

**What you do:**
- Sell cash-secured puts at 45–60 DTE
- Target delta: 0.20 or lower — strictly below obvious technical support levels
- Strike selection: assume the rally fails and the stock revisits a prior consolidation base; price for that scenario
- Close at 50% of maximum profit and do not redeploy at the same level

**Why this works:**
- This is insurance selling, not income farming — the objective is to get paid only if there is genuine panic
- By pricing for a failed rally scenario, you are not dependent on the current elevated level holding
- The low delta and deep OTM strike mean assignment requires a significant downside move — one that would represent genuine value

**When it activates:** Only during Phase 1 when price is elevated by beta or sentiment drift and standard entry criteria are not met. This is the least-bad engagement structure, not the preferred one.

**Rules:**
- Do not treat this as a normal cadence trade — it is a disciplined defensive structure for when patience is the right answer but some engagement is acceptable
- Do not increase size because IV appears attractive at elevated levels — the vol is there because the situation is risky
- If assigned from this strategy, evaluate macro conditions carefully before activating the wheel; do not automatically begin selling calls

**Goal:** Collect a modest premium for accepting deep downside risk on a name you believe in, only at strikes that represent genuine long-term value.

### Strategy 1B — Core Cash-Secured Puts at Preferred Entry (Primary Strategy)

**What you do:**
- Sell cash-secured puts at 30–45 DTE
- Target delta: slightly OTM — approximately 0.20–0.30
- Strike selection: at levels where IV is elevated due to fear or macro contagion rather than fundamental deterioration
- Close at 50% of maximum profit and redeploy into the next cycle

**Why this works:**
- This is where INTC premium selling has historically performed — fear-elevated IV at a sensible price level
- The vol risk premium is at its widest when the market is most nervous about macro or sector contagion
- At a pullback level, the stock's "too important to fail" backstop is most relevant to limiting real downside

**When it activates:** Phase 2 — after a market digestion event or macro-driven vol expansion brings INTC back to a prior consolidation base. This is the primary strategy and the one to wait for.

**Price level to watch — the $60s:**
A pullback into the low-to-mid $60s is a particularly compelling entry window for this strategy. At that level, short put premiums are likely to be significantly elevated — fear and vol compression both contribute — while the probability of the stock drifting meaningfully lower from that range is materially reduced given the structural support of the reshoring thesis and government backstop. The $60s represent a level where the market is essentially pricing in near-failure and the premium reflects that fear. This is the zone where the risk/reward on selling puts tilts sharply in the advisor's favor.

That said, caution is still warranted. If the stock is in or approaching the $60s, something caused it to get there — and that something matters. Before deploying at this level, review all available news and recent developments: earnings results, foundry customer announcements, government contract status, gross margin trajectory, and any macro events that may have accelerated the decline. A $60s entry on a news-driven capitulation is very different from a $60s entry on a slow drift with no fundamental change. Know which one you are in.

**Rules:**
- This trade only works if entry discipline was maintained during Phase 1 — do not deploy here if you already sold puts near the top of the beta rally
- Target fast 50% profit exits to compound the annualized return and redeploy capital
- Monitor volume patterns: distribution days (high volume on declines) signal the pullback may continue; wait for stabilization before increasing size
- If a pullback into the $60s is developing, increase monitoring frequency — check news and developments daily until a position is placed

**Goal:** Capture fear-inflated premium at a price level where assignment represents genuine value and the fundamental thesis provides a real floor.

### Strategy 2 — Covered Calls After Assignment (Wheel — Conditional)

**What you do:**
- If assigned shares from Strategy 1A or 1B, begin selling covered calls at 30–45 DTE only after macro conditions stabilize
- Conservative strikes: delta approximately 0.20
- Avoid selling calls while broader volatility is still expanding — the wheel becomes a grind during active market stress

**Why this works:**
- Once assigned at a value level, covered calls plus any dividend income reduce cost basis over time
- Conservative strikes preserve upside participation if the foundry thesis eventually produces concrete customer wins and triggers a genuine re-rating
- The upside re-rate Intel needs to earn has not yet materialized — do not sell calls at strikes that cap a re-rating event before it has a chance to develop

**When it activates:** Only upon assignment from Strategy 1A or 1B, and only after market volatility is compressing rather than expanding. Do not activate the wheel into a panic.

**Rules:**
- Do not sell covered calls during periods of vol expansion — wait for the market to find footing first
- The thesis upside (foundry customer wins, gross margin normalization) has not earned the right to exist yet; keep call strikes conservative so the position retains exposure to that event
- If macro conditions deteriorate after assignment, prioritize basis reduction over strike optimization

**Goal:** Convert an assigned position into a patient income engine while retaining full exposure to the long-term foundry and manufacturing revival thesis.

---

### Strategy 3 — Vertical Put Spread (Advanced — Fear Events Only)

**What you do:**
- Sell a slightly OTM put and buy a put 10–15% lower as protection
- Only execute when put skew has steepened meaningfully — front-month IV expanding faster than back-month IV
- Net credit must justify the trade; do not execute thin-credit spreads

**Why this works:**
- Defined risk is particularly valuable on a turnaround name with binary-adjacent outcomes — the spread caps the worst case
- Steepening put skew signals fear-driven demand for downside protection that is likely overpriced relative to actual risk
- Allows income participation during high-fear periods without full capital commitment to assignment

**When it activates:** When vol expansion is sharp and put skew is at an extreme — typically during macro scares or sector rotation events that drag INTC down regardless of company-specific news.

**Rules:**
- Do not use as a substitute for Strategy 1B when preferred entry conditions are met — the unhedged short put delivers better credit when assignment is acceptable
- Only execute if skew is genuinely steep; do not force the trade on normal IV levels
- This is appropriate for clients who do not want equity ownership but want exposure to mispriced fear premium

**Goal:** Capture overpriced downside fear during extreme vol events with capped, defined risk.

### How All Strategies Fit Together

| Phase / Market Condition                              | Active Strategy                                          |

| Beta rally, elevated spot, low-volume drift           | Strategy 1A — wide conservative puts only, or do nothing |
| Pullback, fear-elevated IV, prior consolidation level | Strategy 1B — core cash-secured puts at preferred entry  |
| IV spike with steep put skew, assignment not desired  | Strategy 3 — defined-risk vertical put spread            |
| Assigned, macro stabilizing, vol compressing          | Strategy 2 — covered calls to reduce basis               |
| Assigned, macro still unstable, vol expanding         | Hold shares — do not sell calls into active panic        |

**You win if:**
- The stock pulls back to a sensible level, IV expands, and you sell puts into the fear
- The stock goes nowhere after assignment and the wheel compounds the income
- The foundry thesis eventually delivers proof points and the stock re-rates from a low cost basis

You only lose if Intel's strategic relevance collapses — a scenario the US government and domestic supply chain narrative makes structurally unlikely — or if you sell puts at the top of a beta rally and get assigned at inflated prices without a thesis-based floor.

**Position sizing:**
- This is a higher-risk turnaround name relative to HPE or CSCO; size accordingly — smaller positions with more room for error
- Phase 1A engagement should be at reduced size relative to normal cadence trades
- Phase 1B preferred entry trades can be sized more normally given the improved entry level
- Do not over-concentrate; the binary-adjacent nature of foundry execution means individual quarter outcomes can be volatile

## Risk Parameters

- **Max loss (cash-secured put):** Strike price minus premium received; practical risk is assignment at an elevated level into a continued deterioration
- **Max loss (vertical spread):** Width of spread minus net credit received
- **Roll rules:** If short put is tested with more than 14 DTE remaining, evaluate rolling down and out for a net credit; do not roll for a debit unless IV strongly supports it and the Phase 1B preferred entry thesis is intact
- **Exit / stop:** Close or roll on a thesis-breaking event — evidence that the foundry strategy is being abandoned, loss of government support or subsidy, structural gross margin deterioration across multiple consecutive quarters, or a balance sheet event that impairs survival
- **Phase discipline:** The most important risk control on this name is refusing to sell puts at the wrong price level; no roll rule compensates for a bad initial entry during a beta-driven rally

## Thesis Monitoring Checklist

**Thesis intact if:**
- Foundry customer conversations continue even without announced wins
- Government support and reshoring subsidy narrative remains active
- Gross margins showing any directional improvement over a multi-quarter trend
- VIX and broader market vol compressing after a spike — fear window normalizing
- Volume on down days declining relative to up days — distribution absent

**Yellow flags:**
- Low-volume rally continues without any fundamental catalyst — beta drift extending further
- Energy prices accelerating without GDP confirmation — margin compression risk rising
- Front-month IV expanding faster than back-month IV after a price decline — could signal real distribution, not just fear
- Government or subsidy headlines that fail to move the stock — potential sentiment exhaustion

**Thesis broken if:**
- Foundry strategy formally abandoned or scaled back to irrelevance
- Loss of government contract support or CHIPS Act funding reversal
- Gross margins deteriorate structurally across multiple consecutive quarters without a recovery plan
- Balance sheet stress requiring dilutive capital raise

## Key Risks

- **Phase timing risk (primary):** The biggest risk on this name is not the company — it is entry level discipline. Selling puts during a low-volume beta rally near elevated spot prices inverts the risk/reward. The historical edge on INTC lives in the pullback, not the rally.
- **Low-volume rally unwind:** Low-volume rallies unwind faster than they build. When sentiment reverses, vol expands after price falls — not before. Being early means being unpaid and potentially assigned at inflated levels.
- **Macro and energy shock sensitivity:** Semiconductor manufacturing is energy-intensive and capex-heavy. Intel is more exposed to energy price spikes and supply chain disruptions than asset-light peers like AMD or NVIDIA, because it is still rebuilding profitability leverage during a ramp phase. A macro shock compresses Intel's valuation narrative faster than most peers.
- **Foundry execution risk:** No concrete foundry customer proof points outside subsidized demand yet. The thesis requires Intel to win external foundry customers at competitive economics. This has not happened. Until it does, the re-rating narrative is speculative.
- **Gross margin normalization timeline:** Margin recovery is the fundamental prerequisite for the re-rating. It has not yet occurred. Every quarter that gross margins fail to recover extends the timeline and increases the probability that sentiment resets lower.
- **Macro correlation:** INTC is currently behaving more like a beta-sensitive industrial tech stock than a company-specific turnaround. This changes sizing and structure — the stock can move on macro events that have nothing to do with Intel's execution.
