# Wealth Management Journal

This is a living journal of my wealth-preservation philosophy, trading principles, and legacy instructions for my family.

## Purpose
- Preserve a clear record of my values, intent, and decision framework.
- Document how I think about risk, income generation, and long-term stewardship.
- Provide practical guidance for my wife and children if they need to manage assets without me.

## How This Journal Should Be Updated
- Use speech-to-text notes as raw input.
- Keep original intent, but clean grammar and structure.
- Add new entries in reverse-chronological order (newest first).
- Do not delete prior entries unless they are explicitly replaced.
- If a view changes, mark old guidance as superseded and state why.

## Standing Principles
- Capital preservation comes before return maximization.
- Income durability matters more than short-term gains.
- Avoid concentrated, unrecoverable risk.
- Favor repeatable, rules-based decisions.
- Keep family instructions clear enough for non-experts to follow.

### [2026-05-12] KMI Position Size, Discomfort, and Outcome Clarity
**Context:**
I took a large IRA position in KMI before I fully understood the business and became uncomfortable with the position after entry.

**What I observed:**
The stock declined for most of the holding period after my entry, which increased stress. I used options to collect premium and also captured a dividend. Even though I eventually sold the shares at a cost-basis loss, the combined result from premium and dividend was still positive (about $800+). My emotional experience during the trade did not match the actual financial outcome.

**What I believe now:**
Discomfort often comes from oversized positions and weak initial conviction, not only from price movement. I have a repeat pattern where trades that feel bad in the middle still end up profitable, but the emotional cost is high when position size is too large.

**Portfolio implication:**
Position sizing and understanding quality matter as much as ticker selection. A profitable result does not justify entering a position that creates constant stress or consumes too much account capacity.

**Action or rule update:**
- Do not allocate the entire IRA to a single ticker. Easier said than done.
- Require clear thesis confidence before initiating size.
- If conviction is uncertain, start smaller and use options only as support, not as a rescue plan.
- Evaluate outcomes by total P/L (shares + options premium + dividends), not by share price alone.

**Legacy note for family:**
If a position feels too stressful to hold, reduce size and simplify. Preserving emotional control is part of preserving capital.
