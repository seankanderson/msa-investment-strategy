# Materials

**Macro Profile:** A cyclical, globally-linked sector that spans industrial metals, precious metals, chemicals, building materials, and agricultural inputs. Performance is closely tied to global manufacturing activity (especially China), infrastructure spending, commodity supply/demand cycles, and the U.S. dollar (most commodities are dollar-priced, so a weaker dollar lifts materials prices). Within the sector: gold miners (NEM) are counter-cyclical and perform well in recessions and risk-off environments; copper producers (FCX) are the most growth-cyclical (copper is a leading economic indicator); building materials (VMC, MLM) are tied to construction and infrastructure; specialty chemicals (LIN, ECL) are more defensive with pricing power; agricultural chemicals (MOS) are tied to food cycles.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** China manufacturing PMI, global steel and aluminum demand, copper price (economic indicator), gold price (fear/inflation hedge), agricultural commodity prices, U.S. dollar index, infrastructure spending appropriations, battery metal demand (lithium, nickel, cobalt).

**Rate Sensitivity:** Moderate and indirect. Higher rates strengthen the dollar, which pressures commodity prices. Mining capex is debt-funded, so high rates increase costs. However, the primary driver is commodity prices, not rates.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** Very wide dispersion. Gold miners (NEM) and precious metal names carry high IV from commodity price volatility. Battery metals (ALB) have extreme IV from EV adoption uncertainty. Building materials (VMC, MLM) have lower IV with their quasi-monopoly positions. Industrial gas companies (LIN, ECL) carry among the lowest IV in the materials group.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- LIN is the highest-quality materials CSP candidate: industrial gas contracts are long-term and inflation-linked, the business is largely recession-resistant, and IV is elevated modestly above what the business fundamentals would justify. Sell puts on sector-wide Materials selloffs.
- NEM is an excellent wheel candidate specifically because gold price moves create regular 10-15% sector selloffs that are not business-impairment events. When gold sells off from a fear spike and NEM pulls back, sell 45-60 DTE puts to collect the post-spike elevated IV.
- FCX is a high-IV copper play. Copper price declines on China demand fear create the best entry points. Sell defined-risk spreads (the moves can be large) rather than naked puts during Chinese slowdown narratives.
- ALB carries very high IV from EV adoption timing uncertainty. The lithium market will recover but the timeline is genuinely uncertain — use narrow spreads to participate without over-committing capital.
- DOW is a classic trough-cycle CSP candidate: ~6% dividend yield, commodity chemical trough margins, and a history of recovering strongly when the cycle turns. IV is elevated at the cycle trough, exactly when the risk/reward is most favorable.

**Cycle Timing:** Materials lead recoveries when global growth resumes. The primary signal is China's manufacturing PMI turning positive after a contraction, or U.S. infrastructure spending acceleration. Gold miners are counter-trend: they tend to outperform during recessions and geopolitical stress as the gold price benefits from safe-haven demand and dollar weakness. Building materials (VMC, MLM) are best entered when housing permits and infrastructure contract awards pick up.
