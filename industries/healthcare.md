# Healthcare (Pharma, MedTech, Services)

**Macro Profile:** Defensive, non-cyclical. Healthcare spending is largely non-discretionary, making this sector one of the most resilient across all macro regimes. Demand does not contract meaningfully in recessions. Rate-insensitive relative to most sectors. Outperforms late-cycle and during contraction when investors rotate out of growth. Underperforms in strong bull markets when risk appetite favors higher-beta sectors.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** FDA approval calendars, patent cliffs, Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement policy, hospital volumes, M&A activity.

**Rate Sensitivity:** Low. Healthcare is not a rate-sensitive sector. Dividend payers (JNJ, ABT, MRK) provide modest yield support, but the sector does not trade as a bond proxy the way Utilities and REITs do.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** Biotech and clinical-stage names carry persistently high IV due to binary FDA outcomes. Large-cap pharma (MRK, PFE, JNJ) have moderate IV — elevated around earnings and pipeline readouts. Medical device names (ABT, ISRG, ZBH) are lower IV but still spike on quarterly reports.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- Sell cash-secured puts on large-cap pharma (PFE, MRK, BMY) after sector-wide selloffs driven by policy fear (e.g., drug pricing headlines). The underlying businesses are not affected by the noise, but IV spikes create attractive premium.
- PFE with its ~6% dividend yield provides a strong anchor for put-selling — assignment is a reasonable outcome.
- CVS and BAX as turnaround names carry higher IV relative to their business quality; defined-risk spreads are appropriate over naked puts.
- Avoid selling puts on clinical-stage or single-asset pharma names — FDA binary events can gap through any strike.

**Cycle Timing:** Healthcare becomes a rotation target when the broader market peaks or economic data softens. IV tends to increase modestly during macro fear spikes, creating short-window put-selling opportunities on the strongest household names.
