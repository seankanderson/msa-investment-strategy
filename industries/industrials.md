# Industrials

**Macro Profile:** Mid-to-late cycle outperformer. Industrial earnings are driven by corporate capital spending, infrastructure investment, and defense budgets. The sector has meaningful internal dispersion: defense names (LMT) are effectively counter-cyclical because government contracts are multi-year and not affected by GDP swings; rails and logistics (UNP) are more economic-sensitive but defensive within the sector; aerospace and heavy equipment (GE, CAT) are the most cyclical. Infrastructure fiscal spending (IRA, CHIPS Act) has added a policy-driven secular overlay.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** ISM Manufacturing PMI, corporate capex plans, defense budget appropriations, freight volumes, construction and infrastructure spend, aerospace order backlogs.

**Rate Sensitivity:** Moderate. Industrials are not rate-sensitive directly, but their customers' capital expenditure decisions are. High rates delay equipment purchases and construction starts. Defense names are fully insulated from rates.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** Wide dispersion. Defense names (LMT) have low IV — government revenue predictability compresses implied volatility. Heavy equipment and aerospace cyclicals (CAT, GE) have moderate IV with spikes on earnings and economic data. Turnaround names (BA, MMM) carry elevated restructuring premium.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- LMT is an excellent low-risk CSP candidate in any macro environment. Defense budgets grow in recessions and in geopolitical escalations — IV is modest but the business is a consistent compounder. Sell puts on broad market corrections.
- BA is a higher-risk/higher-reward premium-selling candidate. IV is elevated due to production and regulatory uncertainty. Sell defined-risk spreads rather than naked puts; the turnaround is real but the timeline is unpredictable.
- CAT has highly predictable IV spikes: earnings, ISM manufacturing data, and China economic data all move it. Sell 30-45 DTE puts after each macro scare while the backlog remains robust.
- UNP and rails have bond-like characteristics within industrials — low IV, high dividend, reliable volumes. Covered calls and CSPs work well in range-bound markets.

**Cycle Timing:** Add industrial exposure when PMI turns from contraction to expansion (crossing 50). Defense names can be added in any environment when a geopolitical catalyst presents a short-term fear-driven selloff. Avoid heavy equipment at PMI peaks when the sentiment is most bullish — that is when earnings expectations are hardest to beat.
