# Banking (Financials)

**Macro Profile:** The most macro-sensitive major sector. Banking profitability is driven by net interest margin (NIM) — the spread between what banks earn on loans and what they pay on deposits — which is directly controlled by the shape of the yield curve. A steep, upward-sloping yield curve (short rates low, long rates high) is the best environment for bank earnings. A flat or inverted curve compresses NIM severely. Banks also carry credit cycle risk: in recessions, loan losses spike and provisioning drags on earnings. The two risks can partially offset — rate cuts that are meant to stimulate the economy also signal credit stress, creating a mixed signal.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** Federal Funds Rate, 10-2 yield curve spread (NIM predictor), credit card and commercial loan delinquency rates, mortgage origination volumes, M&A and capital markets activity (for money-center banks), loan growth.

**Rate Sensitivity:** Very high and directional. Rising rates with a steepening curve are strongly positive. Rate cuts and curve flattening are negative near-term, though they can eventually stimulate loan demand.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** Moderate IV year-round with significant spikes around earnings (four times per year for major banks) and during banking stress events. The 2023 SVB/regional bank contagion fear was an extreme example — IV on regional bank names spiked to levels that, in hindsight, were exceptional put-selling opportunities for those who could assess the fundamental disconnect from SVB's unique situation.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- JPM and BAC are liquid, high-quality CSP candidates. Sell puts 45-60 DTE on broad market selloffs. The dividend yield provides a floor and assignment is a reasonable income-generating outcome.
- BAC has above-average sensitivity to the long end of the yield curve, creating more IV than JPM — which means slightly better premium but more macro-driven movement risk. Good spread candidate to limit rate sensitivity.
- Regional banks (KEY, RF, WAL) carry elevated IV from regional banking sector nervousness. Spreads work better than naked puts given the tail risk events seen in 2023.
- MUFG is a longer-duration thesis — Japan's rate normalization is a structural decade-long story. Lower IV, use long-dated puts further OTM; this is more of a conviction equity position.

**Cycle Timing:** Enter aggressively when the yield curve is maximally inverted and banking pessimism peaks — that is the optimal CSP entry point. The inversion eventually normalizes, NIMs expand, and provisioning fears that drove the pessimism prove overstated. Reduce exposure when the curve is flat-to-inverted and credit spreads are widening simultaneously.
