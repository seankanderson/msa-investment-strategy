# Sector Reference Guide — Main Street Advisors

This file is the macro strategy overlay and table of contents for MSA's sector stock universe. Each sector has its own file containing the curated stock list and sector-specific options trading notes. This guide answers the higher-order question: **given the current macroeconomic environment, which sectors should be in focus, and what is the appropriate posture for options-based income strategies?**

---

## Macro Cycle Sector Rotation

The table below maps economic phases to sector posture. Use this to orient the portfolio toward sectors where risk/reward is most favorable, and to identify sectors where premium-selling opportunities are likely to be richest.

| Economic Phase | Characteristics | Overweight | Underweight | Primary Signal |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Early Recovery | Rates falling or low, GDP bottoming, unemployment near peak, credit easing | Consumer Discretionary, Banks, Industrials, Materials, REITs | Utilities, Consumer Defensive | Fed pivot, yield curve steepening, credit spreads tightening |
| Mid Expansion | GDP accelerating, corporate earnings growing, capex increasing, rates stable | Software, Semiconductors, Communication Services, Consumer Discretionary, Industrials | Energy, Materials | ISM Manufacturing above 55, earnings beats, rising capex guidance |
| Late Cycle / Peak | Inflation elevated, Fed hiking, yield curve flattening, margins squeezed | Energy, Materials, Consumer Defensive, Healthcare, Industrials (defense) | Software, REITs, Utilities | CPI above 4%, Fed hiking, PMI rolling over from peak |
| Recession / Contraction | GDP negative, unemployment rising, credit tightening, rates being cut | Healthcare, Consumer Defensive, Utilities, REITs (rate-cut anticipation) | Consumer Discretionary, Industrials, Banks, Materials | Unemployment claims rising, PMI below 45, yield curve uninverted |
| Stagflation | Inflation high and GDP stalling simultaneously | Energy, Materials (hard assets), Consumer Defensive (pricing power) | Software, REITs, Utilities, Consumer Discretionary | CPI elevated + GDP prints 0 to negative + unemployment rising |

## Rate Sensitivity Quick Reference

The Federal Funds Rate and the 10-year Treasury yield are the two most important macro inputs for sector allocation.

| Rate Direction | Benefits Most | Hurt Most |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Rates Rising | Banks (NIM expansion), Energy, Insurance | REITs, Utilities, Software (growth), Consumer Discretionary |
| Rates Falling | REITs, Utilities, Consumer Discretionary, Software, Communication (telecom) | Banks (NIM compression near-term) |
| Curve Steepening (short rates fall, long rates stable or rising) | Banks (NIM recovery), Industrials, Financials | --- |
| Curve Flattening / Inversion (short rates near or above long rates) | Defensive sectors (Healthcare, Consumer Defensive, Utilities) | Banks, Cyclicals |
| Dollar Strengthening | --- | Energy (oil priced in USD), Materials (metals), Emerging market-exposed companies |
| Dollar Weakening | Energy, Materials, Multinationals with USD-reported foreign earnings | Domestic importers |

## Sector Index

Each link below goes to the sector's dedicated file containing the full stock table and sector-specific options notes.

---

### [Healthcare](healthcare.md)
Pharma, MedTech, hospital systems, and drug distribution. Non-cyclical defensive sector. Best in late-cycle and recession. Rate-insensitive. IV spikes on FDA events and earnings create recurring CSP opportunities on large-cap pharma names. Dividend yield on turnaround names (PFE, BMY) supports put-selling floors.

---

### [Software](software.md)
Enterprise platforms, cloud infrastructure, AI applications, and cybersecurity. Rate-sensitive growth sector. Best in mid-expansion with falling rates; worst in rate-hike cycles. AI secular tailwind partially decouples top names from the macro cycle. MSFT is the highest-quality CSP candidate in the sector. Turnaround names (ZM, DOCU) carry premium IV relative to their current business stability.

---

### [Semiconductors](semiconductors.md)
Chip design, foundry, equipment, and memory. The most cyclical sector in technology. Deepest drawdowns of any sector; fastest recoveries. AI data center demand has created a secular tailwind overlaid on the traditional cycle. Very high IV across the sector — the best wheel-strategy hunting ground in technology when down-cycles create fear-driven selloffs.

---

### [Industrials](industrials.md)
Aerospace, defense, heavy equipment, rails, automation, and logistics. Mid-to-late cycle with meaningful sub-sector dispersion: defense names are counter-cyclical; rails are defensive-cyclical; heavy equipment is the most GDP-sensitive. Defense names (LMT) offer low-IV, consistent CSP opportunities in any macro environment.

---

### [Consumer Defensive](consumer-defensive.md)
Food, beverages, household products, personal care, and grocery retail. The classic safe harbor. Demand is inelastic, dividends are consistent, and IV is low. Ideal sector for conservative premium-selling strategies (CSPs, covered calls) in retirement accounts. Best held through recessions; tends to lag in strong bull markets.

---

### [Banking](banking.md)
Money-center banks, regional banks, and international banks. Most macro-sensitive sector: NIM, yield curve shape, and credit cycle quality are the three primary earnings drivers. Best entered when yield curve is maximally inverted and banking pessimism peaks. Earnings IV spikes (4x/year) and stress events create opportunistic premium-selling windows on quality names.

---

### [Energy](energy.md)
Oil supermajors, E&P, oilfield services, refining, and midstream pipelines. Late-cycle and inflation-hedge sector. Primarily driven by commodity prices, not economic cycle directly. Supermajors (XOM, CVX) suitable for any macro environment as dividend-growth holds. E&P and services names require commodity price timing. High IV sector-wide; geopolitical events create premium-selling opportunities.

---

### [Consumer Discretionary](consumer-discretionary.md)
E-commerce, home improvement, restaurants, travel, auto, and off-price retail. Most economically sensitive consumer sector; best in early recovery, worst in recessions. High dispersion internally — Amazon and QSR names more durable; autos most cyclical. Turnaround brands (NKE, SBUX) carry elevated IV relative to business quality, making CSP strategies attractive.

---

### [Communication Services](communication-services.md)
Digital advertising platforms, streaming, telecom carriers, and media. Hybrid sector: telecom (T, VZ) is defensive with bond-proxy characteristics; digital advertising (META, GOOGL) is growth-cyclical. Telecom names are among the best CSP candidates for income-focused retirement accounts. META and NFLX carry high IV around earnings — appropriate for spread strategies.

---

### [Real Estate (REITs)](real-estate.md)
Industrial, cell tower, healthcare, net-lease, data center, self-storage, apartment, gaming, and mortgage REITs. Most rate-sensitive equity sector. Best entered when rate hike cycles peak; worst during active tightening. Dividend yields (3-8%) provide meaningful floor support for put-selling. Net-lease names (O, VICI) are ideal CSP candidates after rate-panic selloffs.

---

### [Utilities](utilities.md)
Regulated electric, gas, and water utilities. Classic recession shelter and defensive income sector. Bond-proxy characteristics make the sector rate-sensitive. AI/data center power demand is adding secular growth to the traditional low-growth profile. Very low IV — best for covered calls and conservative CSPs. Enter after Fed pivot signals; avoid during active rate hikes.

---

### [Materials](materials.md)
Industrial gas, specialty chemicals, metals and mining, building materials, fertilizers, and precious metals. Cyclical and globally-linked sector. Gold miners (NEM) are counter-cyclical. Copper (FCX) is a leading economic indicator. Building materials (VMC, MLM) tied to infrastructure and construction. Wide IV dispersion — LIN and ECL are low-IV quality CSP candidates; mining names are high-IV spread candidates.
