# Utilities

**Macro Profile:** The most defensive sector in the market and a classic recession shelter. Utility companies provide essential services (electricity, water, gas) with regulated, predictable rate-base returns. Demand is nearly perfectly inelastic — businesses and households cannot defer their electricity and water bills even in deep recessions. The sector has historically been slow-growth, but AI data center power demand is adding a genuine secular growth component that is beginning to change the profile of the top names. Like REITs, utilities are partial bond proxies and underperform when rates rise. The AI power demand tailwind (NEE, AEP, SO) creates a new layer of earnings growth that was absent in prior decades.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** Federal Funds Rate direction, 10-year Treasury yield (dividend yield competition), regulated rate case outcomes, energy input costs (natural gas, coal, nuclear fuel), renewable energy permitting, data center power procurement agreements.

**Rate Sensitivity:** Very high. Utilities are bond-proxy equities. When the 10-year Treasury yield rises, utility dividend yields become relatively less attractive and multiples compress. When rates fall, utilities re-rate upward. The AI power demand growth narrative has reduced (but not eliminated) this rate sensitivity for the growth-oriented utilities.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** Among the lowest IV in the entire equity market under normal conditions, consistent with the predictable regulated earnings streams. IV spikes modestly on Fed meeting days (rate sensitivity). Turnaround utilities (PCG, EIX) carry elevated IV from regulatory and legal uncertainty. NRG carries above-average IV from its unregulated retail electricity exposure.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- NEE, DUK, and SO are ideal covered call candidates in range-bound markets. The dividend yield provides strong holding income and the options premium on covered calls adds an additional income layer. These names move slowly and predictably — a defined, steady income grind.
- AWK (American Water Works) is the most defensive put-selling candidate in the sector: water utility with a true monopoly, inflation-linked rate increases, and virtually zero earnings variability. IV is very low but assignment is entirely comfortable.
- NRG and EIX carry elevated IV within the utilities context. NRG's retail electricity and Vivint integration creates business model uncertainty that is IV-inflating. Sell spreads at strong support levels.
- PCG and EIX are turnaround stories where IV remains elevated from wildfire litigation memory. The regulatory and legal path is clearer now — sell CSPs at the lower end of the 52-week range for high-probability income.

**Cycle Timing:** Utilities outperform in two clear environments: (1) recession/economic contraction, when investors flee to defensive yield; and (2) the late stages of a rate-hike cycle when the market begins to anticipate cuts. The worst time to own utilities is during aggressive Fed tightening — the sector will consistently lag the market as dividend yields compete unfavorably with rising bond yields. Enter utility positions after the Fed pivots or signals the end of a hiking cycle.
