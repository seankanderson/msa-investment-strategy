# Consumer Defensive (Staples)

**Macro Profile:** The classic safe harbor. Consumer defensive companies sell products people buy regardless of the economy — food, beverages, household cleaning products, personal care. Demand is inelastic, pricing power is strong, and dividends are consistent. The sector outperforms in recessions and late-cycle environments when investors rotate toward capital preservation. It underperforms in strong bull markets when growth sectors absorb investor flows. Dividend Aristocrats in this sector (PG, KO, KMB) have a multi-decade track record of raising payouts through every recession.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** Commodity input costs (grains, oils, packaging), consumer price sensitivity at shelf, private-label competition, grocery channel mix, emerging market penetration, currency translation (many are large multinationals).

**Rate Sensitivity:** Moderate. Consumer defensive stocks are partial bond proxies — high dividend yields attract income investors, so rising rates create mild headwinds as bonds become more competitive. However, the businesses themselves are not rate-sensitive; the multiple may compress slightly, but earnings are stable.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** Among the lowest IV in the entire market. Predictable earnings, stable dividends, and non-cyclical demand compress implied volatility. This is a premium-seller's dream environment: the options pricing is low but so is the actual risk, making risk-adjusted returns on premium selling highly favorable.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- KO, PG, and KMB are the gold standard for cash-secured put strategies in a retirement account context. Stable earnings, growing dividends, and dividend yield floors make assignment an acceptable and potentially welcome outcome. Sell 30-45 DTE puts 5-8% out of the money on any market-wide pullback.
- HSY is a textbook turnaround CSP opportunity: the cocoa price headwind is a commodity input cost that is mean-reverting, not a structural business problem. IV is elevated from the commodity fear — sell puts while the market is extrapolating the headwind.
- KHC and CPB are higher-IV names within the defensive group — sell spreads rather than naked puts given balance sheet leverage concerns.
- COST and WMT have such large share prices that CSPs require substantial capital commitment; use spreads or small lot sizes.

**Cycle Timing:** Consumer defensive should be overweighted when leading indicators (yield curve inversion, falling PMI, rising unemployment claims) signal economic softening. These stocks will lag during the first 12-18 months of a bull market recovery — rotate toward growth when economic expansion is confirmed.
