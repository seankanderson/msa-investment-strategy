# Semiconductors

**Macro Profile:** Among the most cyclical sectors in the entire market. Semiconductor demand is driven by capital expenditure cycles across data centers, automotive, industrial equipment, and consumer electronics — all of which contract simultaneously in downturns, creating brutal inventory corrections. The flip side is that recoveries are sharp and reward investors who enter during maximum pessimism. The AI data center wave (NVDA, ASML, AVGO) has created a powerful secular overlay that is partially decoupling the top names from the traditional cycle.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** Data center capex (hyperscaler budgets), auto production schedules, smartphone upgrade cycles, inventory build/destocking cycles, China trade policy and export controls, memory pricing (DRAM/NAND).

**Rate Sensitivity:** Moderate-to-high. Semis are long-cycle capex businesses. When rates rise, customers delay equipment purchases. Fabless chip companies with high P/E multiples also face valuation compression. Equipment names (ASML, LRCX, KLAC) are the most capex-budget-sensitive.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** The highest IV environment in the technology complex. Cyclical earnings swings, China trade headline risk, and AI hype cycles create extreme option premium across the board. Memory names (MU, WDC) and turnaround names (INTC, ON, STM) carry particularly elevated IV relative to their current earnings power.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- INTC is a textbook wheel candidate: trades near book value, manageable downside from current levels, and IV is elevated from restructuring uncertainty — the premium compensation is high relative to the actual fundamental downside risk.
- MU follows a well-documented memory cycle. When memory prices are at trough and analysts are bearish, IV spikes and creates exceptional CSP entry points. Sell 60-90 DTE puts after inventory correction headlines peak.
- QCOM has a licensing model that insulates it from the worst of the fab cycle. IV is elevated (China risk premium) but the business is more stable than the premium implies.
- Avoid selling naked puts on NVDA at elevated valuations — a multiple correction creates massive dollar exposure even with a normal % move. Use spreads to define risk.
- Equipment names (LRCX, KLAC) are excellent wheel candidates on corrections — they have near-monopoly positions and recover sharply when capex spending resumes.

**Cycle Timing:** The best entry window is when inventory destocking headlines are at maximum and Wall Street is cutting estimates. Sell long-dated puts (60-90 DTE) after the sector has already declined 30-40% from peak. The recovery from a semi-cycle bottom historically delivers some of the best returns in the market.
