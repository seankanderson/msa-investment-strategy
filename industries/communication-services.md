# Communication Services

**Macro Profile:** A hybrid sector with two very different sub-groups. Digital advertising (META, GOOGL, NFLX, TTD) behaves like a growth-cyclical: ad budgets are among the first corporate expenses cut in a recession and among the first to recover in expansion. Telecom infrastructure (T, VZ, TMUS) is defensive and bond-like — subscription revenue is sticky and not economically sensitive. Streaming and media (DIS, NFLX, WBD, PARA) sit in the middle. The sector was created in 2018 when GICS moved Alphabet and Facebook out of technology; as a result, the sector is dominated by those two mega-caps plus telecom, creating an unusual blend of growth and defensive characteristics.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** Digital advertising spend (closely tracks corporate earnings and GDP), subscriber growth (streaming), wireless subscriber net adds, broadband penetration and ARPU, content licensing cycles, live sports rights deals.

**Rate Sensitivity:** Moderate and mixed. Telecom carriers (T, VZ) are high-dividend, rate-sensitive names that trade with partial bond-proxy characteristics — they underperform when rates rise. Advertising platforms (META, GOOGL) are rate-sensitive via multiple compression but are otherwise operationally insulated from rates.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** Very wide dispersion. META and NFLX have some of the highest IV among large-cap stocks, reflecting earnings sensitivity to ad market swings and subscriber data. Telecom (T, VZ) has low IV. Turnaround names (WBD, PARA, SNAP) have very high IV relative to their scale.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- T (AT&T) is among the best CSP candidates in the sector for income-focused retirement accounts: fiber buildout creates a legitimate growth story, the ~5.5% dividend yield anchors the put floor, and IV is moderate enough to produce attractive risk-adjusted premium. A wheel on T generates income from multiple sources simultaneously.
- VZ is similar to T but slightly more defensive with a lower growth component. Excellent covered call or CSP candidate in flat market environments.
- META is the highest-quality aggressive put-selling opportunity in the sector. The business is exceptional but IV is elevated from AI spending uncertainty and regulatory risk. Sell 30-45 DTE spreads after earnings beats are sold off on guidance concerns.
- Avoid naked puts on SNAP, PARA, and WBD — these names can lose 30-50% on a single bad earnings report and the capital at risk does not justify the premium.

**Cycle Timing:** Digital advertising names (META, GOOGL, TTD) lead the market in recovery because ad budgets turn first. Enter these via CSPs when macro pessimism peaks and forward guidance has been maximally cut. Telecom names can be held year-round; they are particularly attractive when rates begin to decline (multiple re-rating) and when the broader market is volatile (flight to dividend yield).
