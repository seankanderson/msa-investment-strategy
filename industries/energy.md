# Energy

**Macro Profile:** A late-cycle and inflation-hedge sector. Energy is primarily driven by commodity prices (oil and natural gas) rather than the broader economic cycle directly. When inflation is running hot, energy companies benefit both from higher product prices and from investor rotation into hard-asset-linked names. Supermajors (XOM, CVX) are defensive within the sector — their diversified operations, conservative balance sheets, and growing dividends make them suitable for any macro phase. E&P names (COP, EOG, DVN, APA) are highly leveraged to commodity prices. Midstream (KMI, WMB) is bond-like — fee-based cash flows insulated from commodity prices. Inverse correlation to the U.S. dollar: a weaker dollar typically lifts oil prices.

**Key Cycle Drivers:** WTI and Brent crude oil prices, natural gas prices, OPEC+ production decisions, U.S. rig count, refining crack spreads, LNG export capacity and demand, geopolitical risk premium.

**Rate Sensitivity:** Low directly. However, energy capex is funded with debt, so high rates slightly increase the cost of production growth. Dividend sustainability (supermajors) is insulated from rate moves. Midstream MLPs and dividend-focused E&P names can trade as yield alternatives, creating modest rate sensitivity.

## Options Trading Notes

**IV Characteristics:** Elevated across the sector due to commodity price volatility. Geopolitical events (Middle East flare-ups, OPEC surprise cuts or production increases) create sharp IV spikes. Supermajors have lower IV than E&P names. Refining names (VLO, PSX) spike on crack-spread data. Midstream (KMI, WMB) have the lowest IV in the group.

**Best Premium-Selling Setups:**
- XOM and CVX are the highest-quality CSP candidates in energy. The dividend yield (3-4%) anchors the floor and IV is reasonable relative to business stability. Sell puts after oil price selloffs driven by demand fear rather than structural oversupply.
- OXY has Berkshire Hathaway's ongoing buyback support, which creates a meaningful floor. IV is elevated from commodity and Buffett-discount dynamics — a good put-selling or wheel candidate.
- E&P names (DVN, APA, AR) have high IV and high beta to oil prices. Use defined-risk spreads to participate in the rebound without the blowout risk of a sustained commodity collapse.
- KMI and WMB are bond-substitute put-selling candidates — lower IV, fee-based cash flows, and growing dividends. Excellent for conservative premium strategies.

**Cycle Timing:** Enter energy via CSPs when oil is oversold and macro sentiment is most bearish. The best entries historically have been when recession fears drive oil below $70/bbl and analysts are cutting price targets. Supermajors are appropriate at any time as a dividend-growth hold; E&P and services names require commodity timing.
