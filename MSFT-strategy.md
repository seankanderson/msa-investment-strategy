# MSFT — Microsoft Corp. Strategy

## Thesis Summary

Microsoft is one of the highest-quality cash-generating businesses on earth — $80B+ quarterly revenue run-rate, enormous free cash flow, and deep enterprise entrenchment across Office, Azure, Windows, and LinkedIn. The stock is currently trading below its ATH not because the business is broken, but because the market is experiencing expectation compression: Azure growth decelerated from ~40% to ~39%, AI CapEx is running at ~$35–38B per quarter, and investors are demanding AI ROI proof now rather than later. None of these factors break the long-term thesis.

The structural AI position is more durable than the market is pricing. Microsoft is no longer simply "betting on OpenAI" — it has positioned itself as the neutral AI toll road. It owns approximately 27% of OpenAI Group post-restructure (marked at ~$135B), has integrated Anthropic models into M365 Copilot, and in cases where OpenAI shifts compute workloads, Microsoft often leases the data centers and resells compute back. Any AI model that wants enterprise distribution, compliance, security, identity, and billing still passes through Microsoft. They are not picking a winner — they are taxing the entire ecosystem.

The options environment makes this unusually attractive right now. 30-day IV is running ~36–38% with IV Rank above 90 and IV Percentile near 98%. Historically MSFT trades with IV in the mid-20s. The current overpricing of premium reflects macro skittishness and AI anxiety, not company-specific existential risk — which is exactly the environment where premium selling works best.

**Why the stock is depressed:**
- Azure growth decelerated marginally; Wall Street wanted acceleration, not normalization
- AI CapEx running ~$35–38B per quarter, temporarily pressuring margins
- OpenAI compute diversification headlines creating moat-erosion narrative
- Broader macro skittishness elevating IV across large-cap tech

**Why the downside is limited from current levels:**
- Business fundamentals unchanged — deeply embedded enterprise software and cloud
- IV materially overpriced relative to realized volatility; premium sellers have structural edge
- AI backlog converting to revenue is a when, not an if
- Assignment at current levels is a long-term asset entry, not a loss

- IV elevated well above historical norms (current 30-day IV ~36–38%, IV Rank 90+, IV Percentile ~98%)
- Stock trading on expectation compression, not fundamental deterioration
- Azure growth holding high-30s; no collapse in cloud demand visible
- No imminent regulatory ruling or structural event that alters enterprise distribution leverage
- Preferred entry: on macro fear spikes or post-earnings overreaction, not chasing strength

## Strategy Mechanics

The overall approach is non-heroic and cash-flow-first: collect structurally overpriced premium on a best-in-class business while the market resolves its AI ROI anxiety. You are not timing a breakout — you are being paid to remain patient while the thesis normalizes.

---

### Strategy 1 — Core Cash-Secured Puts (Foundation)

**What you do:**
- Sell cash-secured puts at 30–45 DTE
- Target delta: ~0.20–0.25
- Strike selection: below recent earnings-reaction lows and below the IV-implied expected move for the period

**Why this works:**
- IV is historically elevated; realized volatility has been materially lower than implied
- Strong institutional support and dividend discipline limit true tail risk
- Assignment converts cleanly into Strategy 2 at a value entry level

**When it activates:** Primary strategy — runs on a continuous cadence.

**Rules:**
- Assignment is not failure; it is a long-term asset entry at a defined price
- Do not sell at strikes you are not genuinely comfortable owning at

**Goal:** Generate steady premium income while the AI sentiment cycle normalizes.

---

### Strategy 2 — Longer-Dated Put Ladder (Conviction Overlay)

**What you do:**
- Sell puts at 6–9 months DTE
- Strike: 15–25% below all-time high
- Stagger expirations quarterly to spread capital deployment

**Why this works:**
- Locks in structurally high IV across a longer window
- Gives the AI normalization thesis time to assert itself without constant rolling
- Deeper strikes provide meaningful buffer given business quality

**When it activates:** Runs alongside Strategy 1 for clients comfortable with delayed capital use or higher-conviction positioning.

**Rules:**
- Use staggered quarterly expirations — do not concentrate all exposure at one expiry
- Size for comfortable assignment at each strike level independently

**Goal:** Capture the current IV premium across a longer duration, maximizing the edge of selling overpriced volatility.

---

### Strategy 3 — Defined-Risk Vertical Put Spread (Advanced)

**What you do:**
- Sell a slightly OTM put and buy a put 5–10% lower as protection
- Only execute when net credit exceeds 35–40% of the spread width

**Why this works:**
- Provides exposure to elevated IV without assignment risk
- Appropriate when a client wants to participate in premium income but not hold MSFT shares

**When it activates:** When IV spikes further on macro fear events and defined-risk is preferred over assignment.

**Rules:**
- Only execute if the net credit clears the 35–40% of width threshold — below that, the risk/reward does not justify the trade
- Not a substitute for Strategy 1 for clients who are comfortable with ownership

**Goal:** Participate in overpriced premium during elevated-fear periods without capital commitment to assignment.

---

### Strategy 4 — Wheel After Assignment (Income Engine)

**What you do:**
- If assigned shares from Strategy 1 or 2, sell covered calls at 30–45 DTE
- Conservative strikes: delta ~0.20–0.30
- Repeat steadily

**Why this works:**
- Premium + dividend continues to reduce cost basis over time
- Conservative strikes preserve upside participation if AI sentiment re-rates the stock
- Boredom and time decay work in your favor while the market argues about AI ROI

**When it activates:** Only upon assignment from Strategy 1 or 2.

**Rules:**
- Avoid aggressive call strikes that cap participation in a potential re-rate
- Let time do the work — precision is not the goal

**Goal:** Convert an assigned equity position into a continuing income engine while maintaining long-term upside exposure.

### How All Four Strategies Fit Together

| Stock under macro fear       | Strategy 3 — defined-risk spread for elevated-IV juice |
| High conviction, longer view | Strategy 2 — longer-dated ladder locking in current IV |
| Assigned from short put      | Strategy 4 — covered calls + dividend to lower basis   |

**You win if:**
- The stock goes up slowly
- The stock goes nowhere
- The stock dips modestly

You only lose if Microsoft's enterprise distribution leverage genuinely collapses — not if Azure growth normalizes or AI ROI takes longer than expected.

**Position sizing:**
- Size all short puts for comfortable assignment — this is a business you want to own at the strike price
- The longer-dated ladder (Strategy 2) requires more capital commitment; size accordingly relative to account
- Spread trades (Strategy 3) can be sized more aggressively given defined risk

## Risk Parameters

- **Max loss (cash-secured put):** Strike price minus premium received; practical risk is assignment at a strike that continues to decline if thesis breaks
- **Max loss (vertical spread):** Width of spread minus net credit received
- **Roll rules:** If short put is tested with more than 14 DTE remaining, evaluate rolling down and out for a net credit; do not roll for a debit unless conviction is high and IV supports it
- **Exit / stop:** Close or roll on a fundamental thesis-breaking event — Azure growth collapsing to low-20s, enterprise AI spending retrenchment, or loss of distribution leverage
- **Prolonged dead-money scenario:** If stock ranges for 6–12 months, the wheel still generates income; do not abandon the strategy because the stock is not moving
