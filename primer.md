# Stock Analysis — Context Primer

## Who I Am and What I Do

I am an advisor at Main Street Advisors (MSA), a fee-only fiduciary RIA focused on retirement preparation. MSA does not manage accounts, execute trades, or hold client funds. My role is to educate and equip clients to take autonomous action in self-directed retirement accounts — primarily ROTH IRAs.

I use options-based income strategies, not buy-and-hold indexing. My core approach is selling option premium on fundamentally sound companies to generate cash flow while building or waiting on a thesis.

---

## Core Strategy Framework

Keep this framework in mind for all analysis. When I ask about a stock, I am evaluating it through these lenses:

**1. Short put / cash-secured put**
Sell out-of-the-money puts to collect premium. The premium compensates for downside risk. If assigned, the position converts to a wheel. This is the primary strategy for most clients. Typical duration: 30–45 DTE for standard cadence; 6–12 months for longer-dated ladders on high-conviction names.

**2. Wheel (covered calls after assignment)**
If assigned shares from a short put, sell out-of-the-money covered calls at conservative strikes (delta ~0.20–0.30) to continue collecting premium and lower cost basis. Avoid strikes that cap upside if a re-rate is possible.

**3. Vertical put spread**
For more advanced clients: sell an ATM or slightly OTM put and buy a cheaper OTM put for defined risk. Appropriate when IV is elevated and assignment is not desired.

**4. Swing thesis overlay**
Sometimes the above strategies are deployed specifically on companies that are temporarily punished or depressed by the market — where the real goal is to earn income while building conviction toward a potential long position that captures the full upside. Think of it as getting paid to do your homework. Examples: BTI in 2023, INTC in 2024–2025, MSFT in 2026.

**5. Earnings premium capture (HIGH RISK — use with extreme caution)**
Selling premium into or around an earnings event to capture the elevated IV that typically inflates option prices before the announcement. IV crush after the event can produce rapid gains on short premium positions. Timing is critical: sell the premium approximately three days before the earnings announcement — close enough to capture the peak IV buildup, but far enough out that analyst estimate revisions and any pre-earnings news flow have had time to settle, reducing the chance of a last-minute surprise. Selling too early sacrifices IV premium and does not adequately compensate the seller for risk. This strategy is only appropriate when all three of the following conditions are met: (1) you have high conviction the company will meet or beat earnings estimates, (2) there is no meaningful risk of negative forward guidance that could punish the stock even on a beat, and (3) your option has sufficient time remaining after the earnings date to recover if the stock moves against you. Do not use this strategy as a standalone speculative trade. It should only be deployed on names where you already have a thesis and would be comfortable holding or wheeling the position if assigned. This is not a core strategy — it is a situational overlay for experienced advisors on high-conviction setups only.

**Key mindset:** These are non-heroic, cash-flow-first strategies. The goal is to be paid to wait, not to time a breakout. Assignment is not failure — it is part of the plan. We are looking for stocks that can drive returns that are higher than S&P 500 index buy+hold strategy through the sale of option premium or swing trades or a combination of both. Generally we want strong short cash-secured put premium on elevated volatility that is misattributed by the market. 

**Trade management — 50% rule:** The target for managing or closing short put positions is when 50% of maximum profit has been realized. At that point, the remaining premium is small relative to the time and capital still at risk. Closing at 50% frees up capital to redeploy into a new position, compounds the annualized return, and eliminates the tail risk of holding through an unexpected move in the final weeks of the trade. This rule applies to standard short put cadence trades. Longer-dated ladder positions and earnings overlay trades may be managed differently based on thesis timeline and position intent.

## What I Need From You in a Stock Analysis Session

When I bring you a ticker, I want you to help me think through:

1. **Is this a viable premium-selling candidate?** — Is the business stable enough that selling puts makes sense? Does it have a dividend? Is IV overpriced relative to realistic risk?

2. **Is there a swing thesis?** — Is the stock temporarily depressed by market sentiment, macro fear, or a specific event — while the underlying business remains sound? What would need to be true for the stock to recover?

3. **What are the real risks?** — Not generic disclaimers. Specific risks that would break the thesis or make selling premium dangerous on this name.

4. **How would I structure the trade?** — Recommend specific strategy mechanics: which strategy (short put, wheel, spread), duration, strike selection, and sizing guidance.

5. **What do I monitor?** — What are the key data points, events, or signals that tell me the thesis is intact or has broken?
