## Section 3 — Turnaround Thesis by Candidate

### NVO — Novo Nordisk

NVO is the highest-conviction swing candidate on this list. The stock has declined from an all-time high of approximately $147 ADR (June 2024) to $44.74 today — a roughly 70 percent decline from peak — driven by two separate but compounding events: the Phase 3 CagriSema combination therapy miss (December 2024, where the drug underperformed Wegovy alone on weight loss) and the accelerating investor narrative that Eli Lilly's tirzepatide (Mounjaro and Zepbound) will permanently displace NVO from the GLP-1 market. The market has committed a category error. The GLP-1 total addressable market is enormous — over 100 million obese and diabetic Americans eligible for therapy, plus growing international adoption — and the clinical reality is that both LLY and NVO will operate as large profitable participants for decades. NVO's Ozempic and Wegovy remain on formulary, market-leading in terms of established physician familiarity, and growing in real-world volume. FCF was $10.7B in 2024 (down from $12.1B peak in 2023, but the decline reflects CapEx expansion to meet demand, not business deterioration) and on track to reach approximately $14-15B annualized in 2025 based on 9M FCF of $10.9B. At a market cap of $167.5B and a P/E of 9.6x, NVO is being valued as if the GLP-1 market is a binary winner-take-all outcome that NVO has already lost. The thesis normalizes this framing to shared-market pricing, meaning NVO's P/E should converge toward 18-22x on normalized earnings — implying substantial upside. The thesis fails only if NVO loses label access, loses significant formulary position, or if a next-generation GLP-1 from a third competitor disrupts both LLY and NVO simultaneously.

### UNH — UnitedHealth Group

UNH is the second-ranked candidate and carries the most ironclad fundamental floor on this list. FCF of $20.7B in 2024 (down from $25.7B peak in 2023, but with TTM recovery trending back toward $21B in 2025) places UNH in a category of rare business durability. The drawdown — roughly 30-34 percent from the 52-week high — reflects a genuine crisis: the December 2024 assassination of CEO Brian Thompson triggered Congressional hearings on prior authorization practices, media-driven reputational damage, and investor anxiety about regulatory intervention in UNH's Optum health technology business. The medical loss ratio elevated in 2024 as the company processed outsized claims, particularly in government-sponsored Medicare Advantage plans. None of these issues represents permanent impairment of the business model. New CEO Andrew Witty has stabilized operations, reduced the volume of prior authorization denials under political scrutiny, and the Optum revenue base (health IT, pharmacy benefit management, clinical care) continues to compound. The thesis is that MLR normalization in 2025-2026 restores earnings trajectory to $25-30 per share range, and that the regulatory risk around prior authorization is already largely priced in at 29.75x an artificially depressed earnings base. Assignment comfort is high: if assigned below $360, the dividend yield and FCF floor make it a comfortable hold while the business stabilizes.

### ABBV — AbbVie

AbbVie is the most FCF-rich name on this list, with $17.8B in 2024 (down from $24.2B peak in 2022 and $22.1B in 2023) — the decline is precisely what the Humira biosimilar erosion thesis predicted, and the important development is that Skyrizi and Rinvoq revenues are already demonstrating meaningful offset. The setup type is inventory/demand normalization: Humira's biosimilar competition launched in 2023, triggering a well-anticipated revenue cliff, but AbbVie's management correctly guided that Skyrizi and Rinvoq would absorb the loss. The absorption is happening. ABBV's dividend (approximately $6.20/share annually at current prices, ~2.9% yield) is well-covered by FCF even at the $17.8B level. The 20-plus percent drawdown from the 52-week high reflects continued investor uncertainty about the pace of Skyrizi/Rinvoq ramp and whether the pipeline (oncology, neuroscience, aesthetics via Botox) is sufficient to sustain 2025-plus growth. The thesis normalizes at the point where ABBV demonstrates back-to-back quarters of total net revenue growth on a post-Humira-cliff baseline — which is measurable and near-term. P/E of 20.79x on an earnings base still recovering from the cliff creates meaningful re-rating potential.

### MRK — Merck

Merck's drawdown is driven by two overlapping concerns: the IRA drug pricing mechanism and Gardasil demand normalization in China. Both are temporary and both have already been substantially absorbed. Keytruda — the world's top-selling cancer drug — remains the primary FCF driver, with 2024 FCF of $18.1B (nearly double the 2023 figure of $9.1B, and above the 2022 figure of $14.7B). The IRA pricing negotiations for Keytruda do not take effect until 2028, giving Merck six-plus quarters of full-price Keytruda revenue. Gardasil China demand declined sharply in 2023-2024 due to a combination of vaccination hesitancy and government purchasing patterns, but China represents a relatively small portion of total Gardasil revenue and the decline is mean-reverting. The pipeline supports the thesis: pembrolizumab (Keytruda) has ongoing Phase 3 label expansions, Winrevair (pulmonary arterial hypertension, acquired via Acceleron) is launching, and the cardiovascular pipeline adds optionality. At P/E of 13.54x on a strong FCF year, Merck is the cheapest earnings yield on this list (excluding NVO on a P/E basis). Assignment comfort is moderate because the IRA mechanism is a real structural headwind in 2028-onward, but the trade window is well inside that risk window.

### AMGN — Amgen

Amgen's drawdown from its 52-week high is driven almost entirely by a single event: the disappointing Phase 2 MariTide obesity drug results (2024), which showed meaningful weight loss but inferior to tirzepatide and semaglutide comparators, triggering investor exit from AMGN's obesity pipeline optionality premium. The core business was never in question. FCF of $10.4B in 2024 (up 41 percent from $7.4B in 2023, with the 2023 trough partially due to Horizon Therapeutics acquisition integration costs) demonstrates durable cash generation. Enbrel is declining as expected due to biosimilar competition, but Repatha (cholesterol), Tezspire (asthma), Evenity (osteoporosis), and Blincyto/Lumakras (oncology) provide a diversified offset. The MariTide story is not over — Phase 3 readouts are anticipated in 2025-2026 and AMGN has the manufacturing and commercial infrastructure to compete in obesity if efficacy improves. At P/E of 16.07x and P/FCF of approximately 18.2x, Amgen carries a discount to its historical range. Assignment comfort is moderate-high: the balance sheet is solid despite elevated debt from the Horizon acquisition, and the dividend is covered by FCF.

### GILD — Gilead Sciences

Gilead presents a more complex setup than the others on this list. The drawdown from the 52-week high reflects disappointment with Trodelvy (sacituzumab govitecan) in oncology — where competitive pressures and label limitations have hampered the once-anticipated blockbuster trajectory — as well as general biotech sector de-rating. The core HIV franchise, anchored by Biktarvy (protected by patents through approximately 2033), continues to generate approximately $10B in annual FCF with high visibility. The thesis has two components: a base case (Biktarvy + Veklury residuals + early oncology revenue) that supports the stock at current levels, and an option value catalyst (Lenacapavir for HIV prevention — the PURPOSE and CAPSTONE trials, which showed near-100 percent HIV prevention efficacy in clinical settings). Lenacapavir, if approved for HIV prevention, would be transformational and represents a real pipeline catalyst rather than speculative hope. FCF was $10.3B in 2024 (up 39 percent from $7.4B in 2023), which strengthens the fundamental floor argument. Assignment comfort is moderate: the oncology pipeline is genuinely uncertain, and catalyst timing on Lenacapavir regulatory decisions extends beyond Q3 2026, meaning the position may need to be held or managed through multiple expiration cycles.
