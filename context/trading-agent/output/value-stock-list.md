## 4. Why Mispriced Now

### MRK — Keytruda Cliff Fear Is Doing Too Much Heavy Lifting

The market is pricing MRK as if the Keytruda patent cliff (2028) is an imminent revenue catastrophe. The TTM P/E of 31.35x looks expensive until you adjust for the $8.265B in unusual charges from the Terns Pharmaceuticals acquisition in Q1 2026. Strip those out and normalized earnings are well above the reported $3.55 TTM EPS. The forward consensus of $9.64 reflects the reversion, and at 11.6x forward P/E, the market is giving zero credit to the pipeline. Winrevair, approved for pulmonary arterial hypertension, generated $525M in Q1 alone. Keytruda is still receiving new priority reviews (bladder cancer perioperative combination). The Terns acquisition adds a CML franchise. The $6.7B paid for Terns is not cheap, but Merck is purchasing pipeline depth ahead of the 2028 cliff, not just running it down. The base case for a rerating: one year of normalized earnings confirm the $9-10 EPS trajectory, and the 2028 concern shifts from an imminent event to a manageable transition. Analyst targets cluster $130-150, implying 17-35% upside from current levels.

### LMT — A Delivery Timing Miss Is Not a Demand Crisis

Lockheed Martin fell sharply in April following a Q1 earnings miss driven by F-16 and C-130 delivery delays, which were production-side timing issues, not demand cancellations. The RSI at 34.10 is approaching oversold on a name whose structural setup has arguably never been stronger. The Iran war has depleted U.S. and allied missile stockpiles; the Army acknowledged PAC-3 MSE shortages publicly. Lockheed received the first contract for PAC-3 MSE accelerated production in April. The Golden Dome ballistic missile defense program carries an estimated $1 trillion in long-term expenditure. A $25.8B weapons push for Middle East NATO allies was approved by Secretary Rubio in May. Defense budget proposals stand at +44% from current levels. The Peru F-16 delay is real but temporary. The Q1 miss pushed the stock 20%+ below its March high while the demand environment for its core products intensified. At 16.1x forward earnings and with a 23% gap to the $636 analyst consensus target, the market is paying a fear premium that the fundamentals do not support.

### CVX — Q1 Reported Earnings Understate Structural Cash Generation

Chevron's TTM P/E of 33x looks expensive but misleads. The distortion comes from a sharp Q1 EPS miss driven by two compounding factors: Berkshire Hathaway's $8B partial sale (reported Q1 overhang), and Chevron-operated assets in the Mideast region facing disruption from the Iran conflict, which temporarily crimped production and elevated stated costs. The forward earnings consensus is $11.95 per share on a PEG of 0.84 — the cheapest forward-growth-adjusted valuation among the energy supermajors. The Q1 beat on earnings estimates (EPS +40.86% above consensus) suggests even with the disruptions, the quality of the portfolio is demonstrating resilience. Oil at current levels (Brent above $100, WTI with Iran war premium) funds Chevron at well above the breakeven needed to cover its $7.14 annual dividend and support its $381B market cap. The dividend has grown for 35+ consecutive years. Berkshire still holds 4.23% of the float. What the market is missing: the Iran war disruption to Chevron's assets is temporary; the structural benefit of higher oil prices to every barrel Chevron produces globally is permanent for as long as the conflict continues.

### COP — Best Balance Sheet in E&P, Cheapest EBITDA Multiple in the Universe

ConocoPhillips trades at an EV/EBITDA of 7.4x — the cheapest absolute multiple among the five candidates. The balance sheet is disciplined by E&P standards (Debt/Equity 0.36), with current ratio 1.29 and ROIC of 8.41%. This is a temporary, geopolitically-driven production shortfall, not a reserve or cost problem. The beat on EPS confirms the underlying discipline. COP has already run 30.77% YTD — the entry timing is less compelling than MRK or LMT, which is why it ranks fourth. However, for an advisor wanting pure-play energy exposure with the best balance sheet and most conservative management in the sector, COP is the correct name. The variable dividend structure (base + excess cash return) means investors participate in upside oil scenarios without the company overcommitting to fixed obligations at cycle peaks.

### PFE — The Deepest Forward Discount in Big Pharma Is Not Fully Irrational, But Is Overcorrected

Third, the Lyme disease vaccine reached pivotal Phase 3 data, offering a regulatory catalyst.
