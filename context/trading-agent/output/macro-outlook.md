## 2. Cycle Call

### Base Case: Late Cycle with Stagflationary Overlay

The macro environment does not fit cleanly into a single phase. The base case is a **Late Cycle** regime with an active **Stagflationary overlay** that is intensifying.

**Late Cycle characteristics present:**
- Growth is still positive but decelerating in real terms.
- Margin pressure is building as input costs (energy, goods) rise faster than consumer pricing power.
- Financial conditions are tightening (bond yields, dollar strength, private credit stress).
- Consumer balance sheets are under pressure from real wage erosion.

**Stagflationary overlay (and strengthening):**
- Inflation is not declining — PPI and CPI are re-accelerating.
- Real wages are negative.
- Oil supply shock is adding a cost-push dimension that monetary policy cannot easily address.
- The Fed cannot cut without risking a further inflation spiral; it cannot hike aggressively without breaking growth.
- Government deficit is larger than GDP — fiscal space to absorb shocks is constrained.

**Cycle Classification: Late Cycle / Early Stagflation**
Conviction: Moderate. The growth leg has not broken clearly yet, but the directional pressure is toward stagflation.

### Alternate Case: Accelerated Recession Risk (if oil > $120 or credit event)

If the Iran conflict escalates to the point of sustained $120+ oil, or if the bond market selloff triggers a credit event (sovereign or private credit), the cycle could skip directly into recession. In a stagflationary recession, traditional defensive playbooks (utilities, REITs) will not provide the same protection because of the rate sensitivity of those sectors.

Probability: 30% within the next two quarters if oil shock continues.

| ------------------------------------------------------ | ---------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Policy regime sensitivity                              | Higher           | New Fed Chair Warsh faces an impossible mandate: inflation re-accelerating while growth decelerates. Prior cycles had either an inflation problem or a growth problem — not both simultaneously with a debt-at-GDP ratio and a fiscal deficit providing no cushion.            |
| Inflation persistence and supply-side constraints      | Higher           | Iran oil shock is a supply-side cost-push event. This is structurally different from demand-pull inflation of 2021-2023, which the Fed could address with rate hikes. Supply-side inflation is harder to break without inflicting severe demand destruction.                   |
| Equity concentration and mega-cap leadership dominance | Higher           | AI theme has concentrated capital in a handful of names (NVIDIA, a few hyperscalers). Market breadth is narrow. Sector performance is already diverging sharply. When leadership cracks, broad index damage is disproportionate.                                               |
| Fiscal impulse and deficit backdrop                    | Higher           | US national debt exceeds GDP. The "term premium" on long bonds is rising — the market is demanding more compensation to hold US government debt. This is a structural constraint that was absent in prior rate cycles (2015-2018, 2022-2023).                                  |
| Credit transmission strength                           | Lower / Impaired | Banks are not aggressively extending credit. Private credit is showing stress. The traditional monetary transmission mechanism — cut rates, loosen credit, stimulate growth — is compromised when inflation prevents cuts and when the debt burden suppresses fiscal stimulus. |
